      Methods, sources and limitations
      • Baseline health access uses the original MOH facility dataset and a 3 km analysis grid: served at 5 km or less, underserved at 5–8 km, and unserved beyond 8 km. Distances also count MOH facilities in neighbouring districts within 20 km of the boundary, so border cells are not penalised; only Dedza facilities are drawn on the map.
      • The priority score weights distance, lack of road access and lack of mapped waterways (baseline 55 / 25 / 20; adjustable with the Priority weights what-if). Border slivers under 1 km² are scored but not ranked.
      • WorldPop 2025 100 m estimates are summed into those baseline health-grid classes; they are modelled population estimates, not a census or school-access measure.
      • School and health distances to settlements are straight-line. Settlement counts are not population-weighted, and the OSM population attribute is present for only a small subset.
      • The 2026 ArcGIS health facilities are a separate reference layer and do not recalculate baseline health coverage.
      • Available water-point extracts contain no Dedza points. Mapped waterways are not water points. Route choices use the mapped road/path network and compare shortest distance with estimated time based on assumed speeds; they do not model traffic, road condition or seasonal closures, and unmapped roads may change results.

      Sources: MOH Malawi baseline facilities; supplied Malawi school dataset; WorldPop 2025 R2025A v1 (CC BY 4.0); ArcGIS Malawi Health 2026 FeatureServer (license/provenance to confirm); OSM settlements, roads and waterways; geoBoundaries Malawi administrative boundaries.
