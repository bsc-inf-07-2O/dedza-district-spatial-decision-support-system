Priority weights

Decide what matters most when siting a new facility: how many people it must serve by 2030, how fast that population is growing, how far people are from care today, whether the area is reachable by a classified road, and whether it is safe from river flooding. Weights are normalised to 100%, and the numbered top-12 priority areas update on the map and in the Priority cells tab.

        Methods, sources and limitations
        • Baseline health access uses the original MOH facility dataset and a 3 km analysis grid: served at 5 km or less, underserved at 5–8 km, and unserved beyond 8 km. Distances also count MOH facilities in neighbouring districts within 20 km of the boundary, so border cells are not penalised; only Dedza facilities are drawn on the map.
        • The priority score weights WorldPop 2025 residents per cell, distance to the nearest facility, lack of road access and lack of mapped waterways (adjustable under Priority weights). Served cells are down-weighted, and border slivers under 1 km² are scored but not ranked.
        • WorldPop 2025 100 m estimates are summed into those baseline health-grid classes; they are modelled population estimates, not a census or school-access measure.
        • Settlements are the mapped GRID3 v3.0 built-up and small settlement areas (50+ buildings, CIESIN 2024); hamlets are excluded. Each has a WorldPop 2025 population estimate summed inside its extent, and a name borrowed from the nearest OpenStreetMap place. School and health distances to settlements are straight-line.
        • The MHFR registry layer (Ministry of Health, operational facilities, including private and CHAM clinics) is a separate reference layer and does not recalculate baseline health coverage. Where it overlaps the MOH baseline, positions are identical.
        • Road travel time runs from each cell's population centre: walking to the nearest mapped road at 5 km/h, then the quickest road route at indicative motorcycle speeds. Flood zones come from the JRC Copernicus EMS GloFAS global river flood hazard map (1-in-100-year, about 90 m), a global model rather than an official national flood map. Candidate sites are also checked for slope (Mapzen Terrain Tiles) and land cover (ESA WorldCover).
        • Available water-point extracts contain no Dedza points. Mapped waterways are not water points. Route choices use the mapped road/path network and compare shortest distance with estimated time based on assumed speeds; they do not model traffic, road condition or seasonal closures, and unmapped roads may change results.

        Sources: MOH Malawi baseline facilities; Malawi schools dataset (Ministry of Education EMIS-matched; coordinates corrected); WorldPop 2025 R2025A v1 (CC BY 4.0); Malawi Health Facility Registry (MHFR, zipatala.health.gov.mw, Ministry of Health); GRID3 Malawi Settlement Extents v3.0 (CIESIN, CC BY-SA); OSM place names, roads and waterways; WorldPop 2020 and 2030 projection R2025A (CC BY 4.0); JRC Copernicus EMS GloFAS global river flood hazard maps v2.1 (ODbL); geoBoundaries Malawi district boundaries; Traditional Authority boundaries from geoBoundaries gbHumanitarian (NSO Malawi via HDX, CC BY 3.0 IGO).
